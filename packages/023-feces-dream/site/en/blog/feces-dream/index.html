The Claim That a Feces Dream Means Money Isn't in the Original Text

2026-08-05 · 6 min read

Dream about feces, and the next move in Korea is to go buy a lottery ticket.

That association is treated as common knowledge. Bring up a dream like this around people and the first question you'll get is "how many tickets did you buy?"

But open the actual historical record, and something a little odd happens. The word "money" isn't in it.

The Original Scene Is "Being Covered In It" — the Phrase Is "Great Windfall"

Let's be precise from here on.

In the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture and other traditional records, the representative example connected to feces is the scene of it covering your body. And the outcome attached to that scene is the phrase "a great windfall."

Two things diverge from the popular version here.

One. The scene is specific. It isn't seeing feces, and it isn't passing by a place where feces happens to be. It's the scene of feces getting on you and covering you. This is a case of the most repellent possible situation flipping into the most auspicious possible reading — a classic reversal in the grammar of traditional dream interpretation, where extreme disgust flips into extreme good fortune.

Two. The result-word is "windfall," not "wealth." A windfall can be money, but it can just as easily be an opportunity, or a person. Over time, in the modern era, this got narrowed specifically down to "money." Exactly when or by whom isn't clear.

So "feces dream equals lottery win" isn't a conclusion drawn from the traditional record — it's closer to something that traditional record went through several rounds of embellishment to become.

Broken Down by Scene

Being Covered In It

This is the scene with the clearest basis in the record.

In traditional interpretation, as noted above, this is read as a great windfall. The more it covered your body, and the more you accepted it rather than avoiding it, the more favorably it was read.

From a modern psychological perspective, the approach is different. A dream where you don't run from an extremely disgusting situation is often read as a state of confronting something you'd rather not deal with. If you didn't feel bad about it after waking, that might be a sign the confrontation is going reasonably well.

Stepping In It

In traditional interpretation, this was commonly read as an unexpected gain or a chance opportunity. The key word is "unexpected" — it comes from outside anything you planned.

From a modern psychological perspective, this reads as a sense of something outside your control. Good or bad, it's a period when something happens that you didn't choose.

The Restroom Dream

The most common scene, and the one where interpretations diverge the most.

In traditional interpretation, the reading splits heavily by situation — whether you resolved things smoothly, whether you couldn't find a stall and wandered, whether someone saw you. The direction flips entirely depending on the details.

From a modern psychological perspective, a much simpler possibility comes first. It may just be your body sending a signal. Sleep research has consistently reported restroom dreams occurring when the bladder is full. It would be a shame to read deep meaning into a dream that happened simply because you needed to use the bathroom while asleep.

Once that possibility is ruled out, and the dream still recurs, some readings connect it to a sense of shame or a violation of privacy.

Avoiding It

In traditional interpretation, there's a view that this represents good fortune slipping away because you avoided it. This belongs to a later strand of dream-interpretation writing, though, and isn't clearly confirmed in the earlier record.

This article notes that distinction deliberately. It's better to say the uncertain parts are uncertain.

Why Modern Psychology Also Links Feces and Money

Here's an interesting coincidence.

Freud connected excrement and money as psychologically related symbols. The idea comes out of his theory of the anal stage — the logic being that the sense of "holding on to something" and "letting something out" links to our sense of possession and property. Western languages still carry idioms linking excrement and money as well.

It's an interesting point that East Asian dream interpretation and Freud arrived at a similar association independently, from entirely different traditions.

But don't misread this. This is not evidence that "so a feces dream really does mean money." Freud's theory isn't adopted as-is within contemporary empirical psychology today. What's genuinely interesting here is only the coincidence that two unrelated symbolic systems landed on a similar association.

Jungian interpretation, by contrast, treats excrement as an image of something that needs to be discarded — unprocessed material that has simply piled up. That reading has nothing to do with money at all.

So, About the Lottery

Go ahead and buy a ticket if you want. That's everyone's own choice, for fun.

But no connection between this dream and winning numbers has ever been confirmed. There's no mention of numbers in the traditional record, and obviously none in psychological research either. The "feces dream lucky numbers" that circulate online were made up by someone.

One thing, though: having a feces dream isn't a reason to spend more than you usually would. The phrase preserved in the record was "a great windfall" — not "a day when spending is fine."

What to Do This Morning

A feces dream tends to feel a little awkward to mention to other people, so it's easy to just let it pass. But it's actually a dream where recording it tends to reveal a subtle pattern.

  • Was the scene being covered in it, seeing it, or stepping in it
  • Did you avoid it, or just take it as it came
  • Did you wake up feeling unpleasant, or surprisingly refreshed

The third question is the most useful one. The same scene can leave a completely different feeling depending on the person.

It's not unusual for the messiest scene to come out as the best color.
