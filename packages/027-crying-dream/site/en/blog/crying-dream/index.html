Dream Symbols

The Louder You Cried, the Better the Omen

2026-08-11 · 10 min read

You probably touched your own face the moment you woke up.

Checking whether you'd really been crying. If the pillow was damp, that's unsettling; if it wasn't, that's strange too. In the dream, your throat had definitely tightened.

And then the whole day feels off — not sad, not quite settled either.

Open an old dream-interpretation text and there turn out to be a surprising number of entries about this scene. But what separates a good reading from a bad one isn't what you'd expect.

Loud Crying Was Read as Joy

The Chinese dream manual Zhougong Jie Meng (Duke of Zhou's Dream Interpretations) devotes an entire section to scenes of crying. Its first two lines read:

  • Crying together with someone else brings a celebration.
  • Crying aloud, letting the sound out, brings joy.

Read that and it's a little disorienting. Crying isn't sad — it's good news?

But that's not unusual for this tradition of texts. Scenes that look bad get flipped into good omens throughout. The entry on dreams in the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (a major reference work on Korean history and culture) lays out this pattern directly: dream interpretation followed two different approaches — reading a dream as a direct symbol, or reading a dream as the opposite of reality.

The crying entries follow the second approach. It's the same grammar as reading a coffin entering the house in a dream about death as a sign that everything will go well.

But Crying Alone in Bed Was a Serious Bad Omen

The same section continues with entries like these.

  • Crying alone, lying in bed, is a serious bad omen.
  • Someone arriving from far away and weeping bitterly is inauspicious.
  • Crying with bared teeth leads to conflict and lawsuits.

That directly contradicts the first two lines. The same act of crying, but the conclusion flips.

What decided the difference? Line the entries up and it becomes visible.

ScenarioReading
Crying together with someone elseAuspicious
Crying aloudAuspicious
Crying alone, lying in bedSeriously inauspicious
Crying with clenched/bared teethConflict

Crying that was let out was good; crying that stayed trapped inside was bad.

It's not about volume — it's about direction. Whether the sound came out, whether someone was nearby, or whether it was swallowed alone in bed.

So the claim that "crying dreams are auspicious" is only half true, and so is "crying dreams are inauspicious." The old list never reduced it to a single verdict.

Who You Cried With Also Mattered

A body of Ming-Qing era dream texts in the Menglin Xuanjie (夢林玄解) tradition breaks this down even further. It reads a scene of weeping differently depending on who else was there.

Parents and children weeping together were read as a sign of a coming family celebration. Siblings weeping together were read as a sign that hands and feet — meaning family members — would scatter apart. A married couple weeping together was read as the cry of a lone wild goose, a sign of loneliness — except for a young couple, for whom the same scene was read as good news. Weeping with a teacher or friend was read as a sign that the bond between you was fading.

The same tears become a celebration in one relationship and a parting in another.

Note the qualifier again — the exception for a young couple. It follows the same pattern as our separate guide to blood dreams, which notes a different reading specifically for someone in the military.

Traditional dream interpretation never assigned one fixed answer to a single scene.

The Korean Record Has No Entry for Crying

Let's be honest here.

The folk-belief dream-interpretation list in the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture organizes its examples into eight categories: the body, human affairs, nature, houses, objects, animals, plants, and other. There's an entry for lying sick in bed, one for seeing a funeral bier, one for paying respects at a mourning house.

But there is no entry for crying.

The closest thing is a single line filed under "other": if a sick person sings, it is not a good sign. That's an entry about sound, but not about crying.

It's a little odd that the most common emotion of all is missing from a list of dozens of entries. It may simply have been left out when a handful of examples were chosen, or crying may have been too ordinary an act to be worth reading as an omen.

Either way, there's no way to confirm the auspiciousness of crying dreams from the Korean folk record itself.

Chunhyang, Imprisoned, Met Others Who Shared Her Fate in a Dream

"Ever cried yourself to sleep?
There's a particular kind of dream that comes on nights like that. And it's actually recorded — in pansori."

The encyclopedia, in its survey of dreams as a subject in traditional art, mentions the "dream song" passage from the pansori (a genre of Korean narrative singing) piece Chunhyangga.

It's the dream of Chunhyang, imprisoned in a cell. In the dream, she travels to a shrine and meets the spirits of Ahwang and Yeoyeong, Nokju, Wang Zhaojun, and Lady Cheok — historical and legendary women — and hears their own stories of injustice.

The encyclopedia notes what makes this dream distinct: a character in crisis and suffering meets historical figures who endured something similar.

It echoes the first line of the old text we saw earlier — crying together with someone else brings a celebration. Chunhyang's dream sits in exactly that spot. Someone imprisoned alone meets people to cry with, in a dream.

From a modern psychological perspective, a scene like this is sometimes read as connected to a sense of not being alone. This is a symbolic reading, though, not a verified explanation.

Simcheong Wiped Away Her Tears Before Stepping Out

There's a record on the opposite side, too.

In the classic tale Simcheongjeon, blind Simcheong's father tells his daughter about a dream on the morning she is to leave for the Indang Sea. Last night, he says, I dreamed you rode off endlessly in a great carriage — and a carriage is something only someone of high status rides, so maybe something good is coming.

Simcheong knows exactly what the dream means. But she tells her father it's a good dream. Then, after wiping away her tear stains, she changes clothes and goes in to say her farewell.

Read against the list above, this is closer to the entry about crying alone in bed — tears swallowed rather than let out.

Set the two stories side by side and what the old list actually separated becomes clearer. Chunhyang cried together with others; Simcheong wiped her tears away alone.

Of course, this is literature, not a dream-interpretation record. But it shows how people of that era placed crying within a larger picture.

The Dream That Left You Feeling Lighter

Sometimes you wake up strangely relieved. And that's what's disorienting enough to send you searching.

The traditional reading lines up with this. The entry for crying aloud sits on the joyful side.

From a modern psychological perspective, this isn't strange either. Dreams don't always attach scenes and emotions logically. A sad scene followed by a lingering sense of relief is not an unusual combination.

So if you woke up feeling lighter, there's no need to second-guess that feeling. The principle that emotion is half the interpretation holds here too.

On the other hand, if you woke up crying and still felt heavy afterward, that's information as well. We won't manufacture an omen from that difference that isn't in the old record, though.

The Dream Where Someone Else Was Crying

This time it wasn't you crying — it was someone else.

The traditional reading depends on who that person was. As above, crying together with someone falls on the celebratory side, while a person arriving from far away weeping bitterly falls on the inauspicious side. There's also a separate entry for the deceased weeping, which reads differently again.

From a modern psychological perspective, seeing someone else cry in a dream is usually read as how you currently hold that person in your mind, rather than as information about them. A figure in a dream is an image assembled from memory.

So there's no need to worry that something has happened to them and reach out in a panic. Check in if you'd like to, but it's probably best to leave the dream itself out of the conversation.

If it was someone who has passed away who was crying, that belongs with our separate guide to dreams about the deceased.

Told Honestly

Lumi

"Search for crying dreams and you'll find claims that they're auspicious, and claims that they're inauspicious.
Both are half right.
What the old list actually separated was whether the sound came out, or whether it was swallowed alone."

Traditional dream interpretation and modern psychology rely on different kinds of evidence.

What the old record confirms is this much: crying aloud or crying together with someone was read favorably, while crying alone in bed or crying with clenched teeth was read as a bad omen. And that isn't prophecy — it's a record of how people of that era read the world around them.

The encyclopedia itself notes, after laying out its dream-interpretation list, that these readings emerged naturally out of many people's experience rather than from any consistent theoretical foundation.

A few things should be said plainly.

A crying dream does not forecast something sad. If anything, the old entries more often say the reverse.

"Crying dreams are auspicious" is only half true. The very same section contains the opposite entry.

A dream about someone else crying isn't information about that person. A figure in a dream is an image assembled from memory.

No verified connection exists between crying dreams and lottery numbers, either.

One more thing. If you've been waking up in tears often lately, or if your mood stays heavy through the day, that's worth mentioning to someone — separately from any dream. Getting support isn't a sign of weakness; it's a common and reasonable thing to do.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a crying dream a good omen? Crying aloud or crying together with someone is recorded on the favorable side. Crying alone in bed, however, is recorded as a serious bad omen.

I woke up crying. That's common. Whether you felt lighter or heavier afterward is the fork in the road — if you felt lighter, there's no need to second-guess that.

No sound came out, just tears. If the missing sound is what stood out most, that may belong more with our separate guide to dreams about losing your voice.

I saw a family member crying. The old entries split by relationship. Parents and children crying together is recorded favorably. Either way, it isn't information about that person.

What does the Korean record say? There is no entry for crying in the examples at all. We won't invent one where the record has none.

I keep waking up crying. Note the interval between occurrences and what was happening around each one — it tends to read clearly later. If it's affecting your daily life, though, talking to someone is a reasonable option.

Waking up having cried is a strange kind of morning to begin with. Your body feels rested, but your emotions feel like they've been working all night.

Let's just accept that as it is.

Today, there are three things worth noting.

  • Did the sound come out, or was it swallowed
  • Who was nearby
  • What was your mood on waking

The first is where the old list drew its sharpest line. And if you track it for a few weeks, it tends to cluster around particular stretches of time.

Writing down a dream you cried through tends to lighten it a little.

It keeps growing while it's still just in your head, but once it's down in two or three sentences, it's smaller than it felt.
