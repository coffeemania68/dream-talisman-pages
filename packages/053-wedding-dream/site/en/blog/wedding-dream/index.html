The Old Records Read Weddings Strangely

The Dreams entry of the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (a major reference work on Korean history and culture), in its folk interpretation catalog under human affairs, includes this line.

A married couple sitting together at a feast means they will divorce.

A celebration, with the couple seated side by side — and the conclusion is divorce.

The object-related section of the same catalog has something similar.

A mirror breaking means a married couple will separate.

That one makes sense — something is breaking. But in the entry above, nothing broke at all. The couple was simply seated together.

This is a pattern that shows up often in traditional interpretation: reversed logic.

The encyclopedia explains that folk interpretation followed two approaches: reading dreams as symbols, and reading dreams as the reverse of reality. The wedding-related entries lean toward the second.

The same catalog includes entries stating that a coffin entering the house means things will go well, and that lying sick in bed means a promotion is coming, for the same reason. A scene that looks good gets reversed, and a scene that looks bad gets reversed too.

A Record That Read Brides and Grooms Differently

The same catalog has this entry as well.

Seeing a woman marry out is unfavorable; seeing a man marry in is favorable.

The reading flips entirely depending on gender.

We'll present this as it stands, but it needs context. In the Joseon era, marriage meant a woman leaving her household and a man receiving someone into his. Leaving and entering were read differently.

The same logic appears in entries about animals — an animal entering the house was read favorably, and one leaving was read unfavorably. That's the same logic of inflow and outflow found in our rat dream and pig dream guides.

But this reflects the marriage system and the position of women at that time. It isn't something to apply directly today, and it shouldn't be.

The record should be introduced as a record. A dream had by a person today should be read by today's standards.

Why Was This Read in Reverse

As covered in our crush dream guide, the encyclopedia adds one explanation here.

Reading a dream as the reverse of reality connects to viewing dreams as a form of psychological compensation.

Compensation means that what didn't happen in reality gets fulfilled in the dream.

Applied to a wedding dream, a direction emerges. Dreaming that you're getting married suggests, more than anything, that you haven't actually gotten married yet — that a wedding is ahead of you, that you've been thinking about marriage, or that something related to it hasn't been settled.

There may have been some real observation behind why people of that era read this entry in reverse.

The Dream of Getting Married

This is the most-searched scene of all.

In traditional interpretation, the picture is as described above. No entry reads it as simply favorable — if anything, entries read it in reverse.

From a modern psychology perspective, marriage is frequently read as an image of a decision and a transition — a scene of making a choice that's hard to undo.

That's why this dream also appears in situations unrelated to marriage — a new job, a move, signing a contract, a major decision. There's an interpretation that it appears when you're facing something irreversible.

What matters here isn't who your partner was, but how you felt at the time.

  • Were you happy
  • Were you dazed
  • Did you want to run

The third answer comes up more often than you'd expect. And it may not be about marriage at all.

The Dream of a Ceremony Gone Wrong

Lumi

"Did your dress not fit? Were you late? Was there no one in the seats?
Those are all the same family.
And this dream — it's genuinely common."

The ceremony happens, but something goes wrong. This type is surprisingly frequent.

  • Your dress or formal wear didn't fit, or was dirty
  • You were late to the ceremony
  • No guests showed up, or only strangers did
  • The ring or something you needed was missing
  • Your partner never appeared
  • You couldn't find the venue

From a modern psychology perspective, this isn't really a wedding dream — it belongs to the category of performance anxiety. The structure is nearly identical to our exam dream guide.

You'll notice the overlap with exam-dream scenes — a blank test, arriving late, being unable to find the exam room. Not being able to find the wedding venue sits in the same place as not being able to find the exam room.

Both are scenes of something going wrong while others are watching.

And there's relevant research on the exam-dream side. Students who dreamed of failing an exam the night before actually performed better the next day, an outcome attributed to a kind of mental rehearsal.

There's no evidence that a wedding-gone-wrong dream predicts an actual problem.

The Dream of Marrying a Stranger

Your partner's identity was unclear, their face wasn't visible, or you can't remember who they were once you wake up.

This turns out to be the most common version of all.

From a modern psychology perspective, dreams frequently fail to render faces with any detail. Without a familiar face to draw on, only an outline gets filled in. Our stranger dream guide covers this in more depth.

So there's little value in trying to figure out who that person was. The focus should be on the event of marriage itself — the partner may just be part of the setting.

The Dream of Marrying an Ex or Someone You Have Feelings For

This is the version that leaves the strongest emotional residue after waking.

This isn't a prophetic dream. It doesn't mean you'll marry that person, and it doesn't mean they're thinking about you.

From a modern psychology perspective, this leans toward the compensation pattern discussed earlier — something unresolved playing out in the dream instead.

We've covered more on this in our ex-lover dream guide and crush dream guide.

One more thing worth adding: some people wake from this dream wanting to reach out to that person. That impulse was created by the dream — it isn't a signal sent by the other person.

The Dream of Attending Someone Else's Wedding

Here, you weren't the one getting married — you were a guest.

In traditional interpretation, the entry mentioned earlier applies: seeing a woman marry out is unfavorable, seeing a man marry in is favorable — a reading written from the guest's point of view. As noted above, though, this reflects the standards of its era.

From a modern psychology perspective, being a guest puts you in the position of an observer, watching someone else's transition.

What matters here is your emotional response.

  • Were you genuinely happy for them
  • Did you feel envious
  • Did you feel left out

If the second or third feeling is what stuck with you, this may be less about the wedding and more about a sense of comparison — especially if you've recently heard a lot of wedding news around you.

Wedding Dreams for the Already Married

It's also common for married people to dream of getting married, and it feels even more unsettling when the partner in the dream isn't their actual spouse.

This isn't prophetic or a signal either.

From a modern psychology perspective, a wedding dream for someone already married is often read as an image of starting over or revisiting a past choice. It doesn't necessarily mean there's a problem in the relationship — it's often that an old major decision resurfaces as material when a new one is approaching.

Some people feel guilty after a dream like this, but dream content isn't something you choose. It's a scene your brain generates, unconnected to your actual will.

If You're Actually Planning a Wedding Right Now

This deserves its own section, since many readers arrive here for exactly this reason.

Dreaming that your wedding falls apart while you're actually planning one is extremely common.

There's a lot to prepare, a lot to decide, and it all happens in front of other people. That pressure becomes the dream, following the same structure as the exam dream discussed above.

This isn't a premonition — it's a rehearsal.

There's even a claim that the harder someone is preparing, the more often they have this dream, which lines up with the direction of the exam-dream research.

That said, if this dream is costing you sleep or raising your anxiety, that's not a dream problem — it's a sign the preparation load itself is too heavy. It may help to trim the schedule or spread the tasks out.

The Honest Answer

Lumi

"You'll find claims both ways — that wedding dreams are auspicious, and that they're inauspicious.
What's actually in the record reads more like 'a couple seated together at a feast means divorce.'
That's not a line to take literally — it's a record of how people back then read things in reverse."

Traditional interpretation and modern psychology rely on different kinds of evidence.

What's confirmed in the old records is that wedding-related scenes were read in reverse, and that brides and grooms were read differently.

The encyclopedia itself, after listing these folk interpretation methods, states directly that such readings formed naturally out of many people's experience rather than from a consistent theoretical basis.

A few points should be made clearly.

A wedding dream is not an omen of marriage. Nor is it an omen of divorce.

The record reading brides and grooms differently reflects the marriage system of its era. It isn't something to apply directly to people today.

A ceremony-gone-wrong dream doesn't predict an actual problem.

Marrying an ex in a dream doesn't predict anything either.

There's no verified connection between this dream and lottery numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a wedding dream a good-omen dream? The old records include no entry that reads it straightforwardly as favorable, and some entries read it in reverse. Modern interpretation reads it as an image of decision or transition.

My wedding fell apart in the dream. A very common type, structurally similar to an exam dream. It reads more as rehearsal than premonition.

I married someone I didn't recognize. Dreams often fail to render faces in detail. The focus belongs on the event of marriage itself, rather than the partner.

I dreamed of marrying an ex. Not a prophetic dream. One reading connects it to compensation — something unresolved plays out in the dream.

I'm married, but I dreamed of marrying someone else. Common, and not a signal. Dream content isn't something you choose, so there's no reason for guilt.

My wedding prep dreams keep going badly. There's a lot to prepare and it all happens in public, so that pressure becomes the dream. It's said to happen more often to people preparing especially hard.

A wedding dream leaves you with an ambiguous feeling once you wake — neither clearly good nor clearly bad.

There's nothing you need to do today. If anything, it's worth checking whether you're facing some hard-to-reverse decision lately. It doesn't have to be marriage.

And if you're actually planning a wedding, just cross one item off your list today and rest.
