Why the Pig Dream Became So Famous

2026-08-05 · 3 min read

In Korea, the pig dream is practically a brand. Bring up dreams in conversation and someone will almost always say, "If you dream about a pig, you'd better buy a lottery ticket."

This dream, though, actually shows up in the record.

What the Original Text Actually Says

The animal-related section of the "Dreams" entry in the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (a major reference work on Korean history and culture) states it plainly: seeing a pig means food is coming.

The "Koreans and Their Dreams" section of the same entry repeats the connection between a pig dream and food, framed as a folk example from ordinary people.

Let's be precise about what this actually says. The original text refers to food, not wealth in general. And notably, it reads this as favorable from simply seeing the pig — no physical contact required.

The idea that a pig dream means a windfall of money is a later development. It's closer to an old-era sensibility — from a time when having food essentially meant having wealth — carried forward and reshaped over the generations.

Same Pig Dream, Different Readings

The same entry includes an entertaining piece of oral folklore.

A man brought the same pig dream to a dream interpreter three separate times, and each time got a different answer: food the first time, clothing the second, and a beating the third.

This tells us that people back then already understood that a pig dream doesn't resolve to just one meaning.

So the popular formula "pig dream equals jackpot" doesn't match the attitude of the original source.

What Kind of Pig Was It?

The texts don't break this down explicitly, but sorting the scene out yourself is worth doing for later reference.

The pig was simply there — the only form the original text directly supports. Simply seeing it has long been read as favorable.

The pig followed you, or came into your arms — later interpretations treat this as the best possible version of the scene. That said, there's no confirmed textual basis for it.

Several piglets appeared — there's a school of thought that more is better, though the evidence here is mixed as well.

The pig was threatening — this is closer to the "beating" outcome from the folktale above. It isn't read as purely favorable.

A Modern Psychological Perspective

Modern psychology doesn't connect pigs to any specific outcome. Instead, it treats the size of the animal in the dream and how it approached you as a reflection of your current emotional state.

Did it come close, stay at a distance, or run away? From this angle, distance and direction carry more information than the animal itself.

The Most Honest Answer

Lumi

"I'm guessing the lottery question is coming, so let me get ahead of it.
There's no connection between a pig dream and specific numbers.
Not in traditional records, and not in psychological research either."

One more thing worth knowing. Open the encyclopedia's separate "Pig" entry, and there's no mention of dreams at all. In the 2024 revision, the entry was reworked around livestock science and food science, and the dream-related material was removed entirely.

You wouldn't know that without checking directly. That's exactly why we check the "Dreams" entry first, rather than relying on an animal's individual entry.
