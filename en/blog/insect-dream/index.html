Insects Were Never One Category

2026-08-12 · 10 min read

You probably brushed yourself off the moment you woke up.

Insect dreams leave a sensation behind — a crawling feeling, the sense of something clinging to you, a faint scuttling sound. And almost everyone searching for this dream feels the same way about it. Unsettled.

Open an old dream manual, though, and it's a little bewildering. Insects were never treated as one category. Each species gets its own entry, and the conclusions go in every direction.

The Conclusion Changes by Species

The old Chinese dream manual Zhougong Jie Meng (the Duke of Zhou's dream manual) has an entire chapter devoted to insects. Gathering its lines together produces this.

  • A bee stinging your foot brings the joy of wealth
  • A centipede biting you means a long life — auspicious
  • An earthworm brings great fortune to fields and to the household
  • A pair of dragonflies flying toward each other means a beautiful person is coming
  • A mosquito bite means you will lose something
  • A fly landing on your clothes always means slander is coming
  • Dung beetles gathering in a heap means you will lose wealth
  • Bats flying in a swarm means a secret matter will drag on

Read through it, and no single rule emerges. Being stung or bitten leads to both good and bad outcomes.

A bee sting brings wealth, a centipede bite brings long life, but a mosquito bite means loss.

So a single search term like "insect dream" won't get you very far. The old catalog simply isn't organized that way.

If you remember which insect it was, that's the single most useful piece of information this dream carries.

The "Dreams" entry of the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (a major reference work on Korean history and culture) doesn't include a confirmed independent entry for insects or spiders in its folk dream-interpretation catalog. The passages above come from a Chinese text, in a different lineage from the Korean list.

A Spider Entry Isn't Confirmed

Let's be honest here.

Spiders are the most-searched insect of all, yet we couldn't clearly confirm a spider entry in the publicly available original text.

Bees, centipedes, earthworms, mosquitoes, flies, dragonflies, and dung beetles are all there — spiders aren't among them. There may be an entry in some edition, but we didn't have the means to cross-check across versions.

So a spider dream's fortune can't be traced to the old records. Modern dream-reading sites make various claims about it, but we couldn't confirm any original-text basis.

The modern side is thin here too. We found no study directly comparing whether people with a spider phobia dream about spiders more often. It sounds plausible, but there's no measured data behind it.

We won't write something in as though it exists when it doesn't.

The Modern Research Here Is Thin

This topic has fewer confirmed figures than most of our other guides. So let's start with what's missing.

The general-population rate of insect dreams isn't known. No large-sample study appears to have counted insects as their own category.

Whether one insect differs in meaning from a swarm hasn't been verified either. No study confirms whether dream content or emotional tone changes with the number of insects present.

The relationship between disgust sensitivity and insect dreams also isn't quantified.

The claim that "insects coming out of your body means suppressed feelings are being released" has no academic backing. It's widely repeated, but unconfirmed.

Here's what is confirmed. A literature review on insects and dreams discusses cases where insects actually appear in dreams and their cultural interpretations, and touches on how illness, fever, and environmental stimuli can influence dreams. That same literature, though, doesn't provide frequency statistics.

The hypothesis that dreams rehearse threatening situations in advance does address animals and being chased, but it hasn't specifically tested the frequency or symbolism of insect dreams.

So this article can't answer "how common is this dream."

What Was the Scene

This dream branches into quite a few distinct scenes.

A Single Insect Crawling

On the wall, on the floor, or on your own body.

In traditional interpretation, as shown above, this splits by species. An earthworm is recorded as greatly auspicious; a fly is recorded in connection with slander.

You Were Bitten or Stung

In traditional interpretation, this is where the split is finest. A bee sting means wealth, a centipede bite means long life, a mosquito bite means loss.

Three entirely different readings for the same act of being "bitten."

If the physical sensation of being bitten stood out, our blood dream guide or injury dream guide are worth a look too — we cover there that pain shows up in actual dream logs at a rate as low as 0.59%.

A Swarm Descended

The most frightening version to remember.

In traditional interpretation, a few entries deal with groups. Dung beetles gathering in a heap means lost wealth, and bats swarming means a secret matter will drag on.

But not every insect swarm can be lumped together. Even those entries specify particular species.

Read in a modern light, an overwhelming number is often read as a matter of being unable to cope. One is manageable to look at; a hundred isn't. We made the same point in our bird dream guide.

That said, no study has verified a difference between one insect and a swarm. This is interpretation, not a finding.

Insects Came Out of Your Body

The type that lingers longest after waking.

In traditional interpretation, there are relevant lines here too. Falling ill and growing insects on the body brings a military post, and insects moving across the body means an illness will ease.

Both are favorable. But the modern reading of "coming out and being released" doesn't appear in the original text. That interpretation was added later.

You Killed the Insect

In traditional interpretation, an entry for killing an insect is hard to find. Entries for catching other animals are plentiful; insects are the exception.

Read in a modern light, actively removing a threat is sometimes connected to a sense of control. This isn't verified, though.

It Was on Your Clothes or Food

In traditional interpretation, there's a line stating that a fly landing on your clothes always brings slander.

If the clothing itself stood out, our clothes dream guide is worth a look too.

You Only Heard It

Nothing was visible, but something seemed to be there.

In traditional interpretation, this scene has no entry. Read in a modern light, this is less about the insect and more a scene about something unseen.

There May Have Actually Been Something There

This is worth noting.

The observation that whatever touches the body during sleep bleeds into a dream is an old one. Yi Ik (이익, a late-Joseon scholar of the Silhak, or Practical Learning, movement), made the same observation in his essay Monggamnon: what touches the body during sleep becomes part of the dream.

So any of these could have been the material.

  • You were actually itchy somewhere
  • Your blanket or clothing brushed against your skin
  • You saw or were bitten by an insect during the day
  • You had some worry related to pest control

None of these can be confirmed as the definite cause, though.

And the literature review mentioned earlier also discusses how illness or fever can influence dreams — the idea being that unpleasant sensory dreams increase when you're not feeling well, though this isn't established specifically for insect dreams either.

If your skin is genuinely itchy or feels off lately, that's worth checking separately from the dream.

The Most Honest Answer

"Search for insect dreams and you'll find plenty of claims that it's a bad omen.
The old entries say a bee sting means wealth and a centipede bite means long life.
Insects were never treated as one lump category."

Traditional dream interpretation and modern research rely on different kinds of evidence, and this topic is especially thin on the modern side.

What's confirmed. Old dream manuals devote an entire chapter to insects, with separate entries by species reaching very different conclusions. Bees, centipedes, and earthworms fall on the favorable side; mosquitoes, flies, and dung beetles fall on the unfavorable side. There are also favorable entries for insects appearing on the body.

What isn't confirmed. The general-population rate of insect dreams is unknown. A spider entry couldn't be confirmed in the original text. The relationship between spider phobia and spider dreams hasn't been studied. The difference between one insect and a swarm hasn't been verified. A fixed entry for insect dreams isn't confirmed in the Korean folk catalog either.

The encyclopedia itself notes, after laying out its list of dream-reading methods, that such interpretations formed naturally from many people's experiences rather than from a consistent theoretical basis.

A few points should be made clearly.

There's no basis for calling an insect dream a bad omen. Several old entries fall on the favorable side.

The claim that "one insect is a small worry, a swarm is a big worry" has never been verified.

The reading that "insects coming out of your body is an auspicious dream about a problem clearing out" also doesn't appear in the original text. It was added later.

An insect dream doesn't diagnose an illness, either. If something is actually bothering you, that's a separate matter from the dream.

There's no verified connection between an insect dream and lottery numbers, either.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an insect dream a bad omen? The old catalog varies by species. A bee sting means wealth, a centipede bite means long life, a mosquito bite means loss.

I dreamed about a spider. A spider entry couldn't be confirmed in the public original text. There's also no research on the relationship between spider phobia and spider dreams.

Insects came in a swarm. There are a few entries about groups, but they specify particular species. Not every insect swarm can be lumped into one reading.

Insects came out of my body. The old entries include favorable lines about insects appearing on the body. But the reading of "coming out and being resolved" doesn't appear in the original text.

I was bitten and it hurt. Pain registering in a dream is relatively rare — reported at around 0.59% in actual dream logs.

How common is this dream? Unknown. No representative study appears to have counted insects as their own category.

Could there actually be an insect nearby? The dream itself won't tell you. But if something feels itchy or off, that's worth checking separately.

Insect dreams stick with you because of the sensation. It isn't especially dangerous, yet the body reacts first anyway.

But the old catalog never asked about that sensation. It asked one thing only: what kind of insect was it.

That's the biggest difference from how we search today. We type "insect dream," while people of that era filed bees and mosquitoes under separate entries entirely.

Three things worth writing down today:

  • What kind of insect it was — the one question the old catalog actually asked
  • Whether it was one, or several
  • Whether it touched you

If you remember the first, that's the most useful detail. If not, the second and third are enough on their own.
